Beauty Advisor Sales Tracker

We already licensed a platform for this. It did not fit my market, so I built the replacement in two days and got 18 advisors onto it without anyone being told to.

READ THE CASE STUDY · RUN THE DEDUP +

PROBLEM

Egypt was my account and it was growing about 6x over 18 months. The advisors were sending me their daily sales over WhatsApp and I was retyping them into Excel every evening. That does not survive an account growing at that rate.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS

The obvious answer was to put the market on the field sales platform we already licensed. It would not have helped. Loading a monthly target into it meant emailing the vendor's IT team and waiting, so the person responsible for the numbers could not change her own targets. The dashboard showed what the vendor had decided a dashboard shows. And the per seat cost could not be justified at that market size anyway. We did not have a missing tool. We had a tool that nobody on my side could operate, and buying more of it would not have fixed that. So I did not go and ask for the budget.

BUILD

Two days from deciding to a running site, with Supabase behind it, which I learned on this project. The advisors are on their phones all day, so instead of shipping a native app I had them add the site to their home screen from the browser. Then I gave it to three advisors and the sales supervisor for a week before anyone else saw it. The supervisor got the dashboard with commissions and targets calculating themselves, which was the part she had been doing by hand. I took their feedback, fixed what they hated, and only then launched it to everyone. January to March I loaded myself by writing SQL against Supabase, because the app only went live in April.

PROOF

I had backfilled everything up to launch, and then advisors came online on different dates because of the signup problem. While somebody was still locked out I was entering their sales for them. When they got in they started logging their own, and they could not see what was already in there, so they re-entered days I had already covered. The dedup key is what decides whether two rows are one event or two. Here are two that look like two days of work and are actually one. The highlighted columns are the key. Run it.

WHAT BROKE

The signup. Not the app, the signup. I had built the thing and tested it and it worked, and then advisors could not get in, because not everyone has an email they can actually access and not everyone has ever registered for anything. That is the part I did not see coming, and it had nothing to do with the product. 02 / PERSONAL BUILD

Move-Out Sale Site & Tracker

One Python script generates the buyer-facing catalog and the internal profit tracker from a single item list, so the listing and the accounting cannot disagree.

READ THE CASE STUDY +

PROBLEM

Thirty one pieces of furniture to sell before I left the country, and every buyer asked the same three questions about size, condition and whether the price moved. One thread each, the same answers retyped.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS

A catalog fixes the messaging. It does nothing about the part that was actually costing me money. I was pricing against store catalog pages rather than against what I had paid, and those pages were wrong by more than double. A dresser I had recorded at AED 1,100 had cost 499, so the site was advertising a saving that did not exist. The real problem was that the listing and the accounting were two separate documents, free to disagree with each other.

BUILD

The generator prices against purchase price, not the store catalog page, so a saving cannot be displayed unless it is real. Bundles carry their cost on the bundle line, with no invented per-item split, and unallocated cost stays visible instead of being quietly spread around.

PROOF

The live catalog, generated from the same item list that feeds the profit tracker.

WHAT BROKE

A dresser went live with a discount badge on it that was not real. I had it recorded at AED 1,100 when it actually cost 499, because I had trusted a store catalog page instead of the receipt. That is the whole reason the generator now prices against what was paid. There is also a bug on the catalog right now: it renders 0 items available from a hardcoded placeholder that the JavaScript overwrites on a normal load. It should say 11. I would rather tell you than have you find it.

IMPACT

20 of 31 items sold in 30 days. AED 8,400 recovered against AED 10,688 paid for those items, a 78.6% recovery rate. AED 4,900 still on the table across 11 items.

03 / AI PRODUCT · HACKATHON 2026

Homebase

A deterministic context block that helps a voice agent dispatch maintenance vendors. Structured by default, with the model behind an explicit opt-in flag.

READ THE CASE STUDY · TRY THE GENERATOR +

PROBLEM

Dispatching a maintenance vendor by phone means a human assembling the same context every time: what is broken, which unit, when the tenant is free, and what else that vendor already has booked. Assembled ad hoc, it comes out differently on every call and details get dropped.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY WAS

The obvious answer is to put a model on the call and let it write the script. But the variation was never the useful part. The inconsistency was the entire problem, and what a vendor needs on the other end of the phone is the same eight fields in the same order every time. That is a template problem, not a model problem. It is why the default path is a plain string builder and the model only runs behind a flag.

BUILD

Eight fixed fields, assembled per call so nothing is left to whoever is on the phone. The LLM path only runs with USE_LLM_SCOPE=true; the default is a plain template builder, because a string template was sufficient for the job.

PROOF

Change the ticket. The block keeps its shape, because the shape is the product.

build_context() · deterministic path

Same eight fields every call, whatever you pick. The system prompt never changes; only this block does.

WHAT BROKE

Most of it, in the sense that most of it never shipped. What exists after roughly three and a half hours is a CRUD app over six Firestore collections with seed data, no auth and test mode rules. The RERA compliance layer the README describes is listed as out of scope in that same README, so it is a specification and not a product, and I am not going to call it one. The concept page also advertises tenant satisfaction and response time figures for a build with no production usage. Those are not mine, they are not repeated here, and the screenshot above is cropped above them on purpose.

IMPACT

Hackathon build, co-founded, no production usage, so there is no impact number to claim and I am not inventing one. The decision worth showing instead: the voice agent's system prompt stays static while per-call variation is injected as a structured block, so the prompt is not rewritten on every call. The default path is a plain template, with the model only behind a flag, because a string template was sufficient.

01 / THE WALLS

Five reasons the software did not get in.

Every one of these stopped something real. The second row of each is the part I would want to read if I were selling into a company like the one I just left.

01 · THE LAPTOP SAYS NO +

THE WALL

AI tools are blocked at the device level. There are real privacy and security reasons for that and I am not going to pretend there are not. The effect is still that somebody who can see exactly what needs building cannot build it on the machine she was given. So the work moves to personal hardware and personal time, which means the company gets the output and avoids none of the risk it thought it was managing.

WHAT WOULD HAVE GOTTEN THROUGH

Nothing that starts by asking IT to unblock a domain. What gets through is a deployment that never needs install rights, or a pilot scoped so tightly that security is reviewing one workflow instead of a whole category of tool. Sell to the person whose numbers are wrong. Not to the person whose job is to say no.

02 · THE LICENSED TOOL WAS THE CEILING +

THE WALL

We already paid for a field sales platform. Loading a monthly target into it meant writing an email to the vendor's IT team and waiting for somebody there to do it. So the person who owned the account, whose targets they were, could not change her own targets. And the dashboard showed what the vendor had decided a dashboard should show, which was not what I needed to see.

WHAT WOULD HAVE GOTTEN THROUGH

Let the account owner configure the thing without raising a ticket. Every hour of vendor side turnaround on a routine change is an hour of somebody quietly rebuilding you in Excel. That is not a feature request. That is how you lose an account, and it is how you lost mine.

03 · PER SEAT PRICING KILLS THE MARKETS THAT NEED IT +

THE WALL

The platform made sense across the large markets. Mine had a fraction of that headcount and a much lower cost base, so the per seat maths could not be justified for it. Which means the smallest markets, the ones with the least support and the most manual work, get the worst tooling. The place that needed it most was the place that could least afford it.

WHAT WOULD HAVE GOTTEN THROUGH

Price the small deployments like you actually want them. A market that size is not a rounding error, it is the pilot that proves the rollout, and it is where the person who would have championed you internally is currently doing the whole thing by hand at midnight.

04 · THE USER COULD NOT SIGN UP +

THE WALL

This is the one I got wrong myself, so it is the one I trust most. I built the tool, tested it, and it worked fine. Then the advisors could not get in. Registering needed an email address, and not everyone has an email account they can actually get into, and plenty of people have never signed up for anything online in their life. Nothing was broken. I had just never thought to check whether the people I built it for could reach the login screen.

WHAT WOULD HAVE GOTTEN THROUGH

Count the first screen as part of the product. If onboarding assumes an email, an app store account, an SSO identity or a password manager, you have already cut out a chunk of the field team you are being paid to reach. And do not force the cutover. I kept WhatsApp running alongside for a month and reconciled the two by hand, which was tedious, and which is also the only reason people came across at all.

05 · THE ASK IS THE BARRIER +

THE WALL

I never went to management for the budget. Not because they would have said no, but because the ask was more expensive than the build. Making the case for tool spend on a small market means a business case, a procurement conversation, a security review and months of waiting. Two days of building was cheaper than two weeks of asking, so I built.

WHAT WOULD HAVE GOTTEN THROUGH

Understand that your competition is not the other vendor. It is somebody with a free afternoon and an AI coding tool working out that it is faster to build it than to ask you for it. That person now exists inside every enterprise, including the ones that block AI on the laptops. Scope and price for her, because she is the one who already knows what the product should do.

03 / WHAT I THINK

They do not work for the brand. They work for you.

I had 55 beauty advisors under me and I was responsible for their salaries, their commissions, and whether they were motivated enough to get through a shift. That last one is not a soft thing. If somebody on that floor is having a bad month their sales change, and that changes my numbers. You feel it inside a week.

It took me a while to understand what was actually going on. They were not working for the brand. Everybody loves the brand, that is why they applied. But nobody stays late for a logo. They work for the person in front of them, and if they do not like that person they will do exactly what the job description says and not one thing more. No matter how much they love the brand, they will not be able to work.

Which is why I do not think adoption is a product problem.

When I put the tracker in front of eighteen people, nobody had told them to use it. No mandate, no deadline, no manager checking. They used it because I had spent two years building the kind of relationship where if I said this will make your life easier, they believed me enough to try it once. The product then had to be good enough not to waste that. But the product is not what got them to open it the first time.

That is the part I think transfers. Forward deployed work means shipping to people who do not report to you, inside a company that is not yours, who already have a full job. You cannot mandate them. You can only be the person whose thing is worth trying. Every decision on that rollout came out of this: the home screen instead of the app store, the month of WhatsApp running alongside, refusing to set a cutover date. None of that is clever engineering. It is just what it costs to not burn the goodwill I already had.

QUESTIONS & CAVEATS

Your questions answered directly.

Written by me. These are the questions that actually come up when someone reads the site, including the uncomfortable ones about how small the numbers are and what the AI work actually is.

01. What did you actually build across these projects? +

Beauty Advisor Sales Tracker: A PWA for Dolce & Gabbana Beauty in travel retail MEA where 18 advisors log daily sales, backed by an ingest pipeline that de-duplicates exports before KPIs are calculated.

LVMH Sell-in Tool: Internal product owner for Givenchy & Kenzo sell-in decision support tool, cutting ordering time from 45 mins to 5 mins across 30+ locations.

Dolce & Gabbana Account Health: Benchmarking layer tracking a 13-market portfolio that flags at-risk accounts early.

Move-Out Sale Site: A Python generator producing a buyer-facing catalog and internal profit tracker from a single source list.

02. Why did you build this instead of using the tool you already had? +

Egypt was my account and it was growing, roughly 6x over about 18 months. The advisors were sending me their daily sales over WhatsApp and I was retyping them into Excel by hand.

We already licensed a field-sales platform. It worked for the large markets. It did not work for mine. Loading a monthly target meant emailing the vendor's IT team and waiting for someone there to do it. The dashboard did not show me what I needed to see. And the per-seat cost could not be justified for a market that size.

So I did not escalate for budget. I built the replacement. Two days from the decision to a running site, with Supabase behind it, which I learned on this project.

That was a judgement call, not a mandate. Nobody assigned it, nobody was waiting for it and there was no brief. It was me deciding, as the person who owned the account, that the licensed tool was the wrong fit and that I would rather build than keep waiting on it. Worth saying plainly, because being handed a project is a different thing.

03. What is the dedup key and why does it matter? +

The dedup key defines what makes two rows the same event rather than two events. Here it was advisor + date + store + shift.

Where the duplicates came from matters more than the key. I had loaded everything up to launch, and then advisors registered on different dates because of the signup problem. While an advisor was locked out I entered their sales myself. Once they got in they started logging their own, with no visibility into what was already there, so they re-entered days I had covered and occasionally skipped days I then had to add. Every advisor had a different start date, so I was tracking that overlap person by person.

Duplicate rows double-count sales and inflate Days Worked, distorting every daily KPI. Settling this key upfront meant that across 1,324 ingested rows, 29 duplicates were removed and zero rows were rejected.

The honest read is that this was a product gap, not a data hygiene one. If the advisors could have seen what the system already held for them, most of those 29 rows would never have been typed.

04. What went wrong in the rollout? +

The signup screen, and I did not see it coming.

The build was not the hard part. The advisors work off their phones, so rather than ship a native app I had them add the site to their home screen from the browser. I piloted with 3 advisors and the sales supervisor for about a week, took their feedback, improved the app, then launched to everyone.

Then people could not get in. Registering needed an email address. Not everyone had an email account they could actually get into, and not everyone had signed up for something online before. It was not that the app was faulty. It was more like, not everyone knows how to access emails, not everyone knows how to register somewhere.

I did not force the change. I kept WhatsApp running alongside the app for a month, so anyone who could not get through registration yet could still send their numbers the way they always had, and then I reconciled the two channels so nothing was counted twice. People moved across as they got set up, not because a cutoff date made them.

Nothing was broken. I had just never thought to check whether the people I built it for could reach the login screen.

05. Isn't 18 beauty advisors a small number? +

Yes, 18 is a small number and I am not going to dress it up. What matters is that nobody was required to use it.

The tracker was optional. All 18 advisors across Cairo, Sharm, and Hurghada logged into it daily because it solved a real friction point. Mandated tools show compliance; optional tools that teams open daily show real product adoption.

18 live, about 20 onboarded. The January to April export holds 18 advisors. Onboarding and training covered roughly 20 field users, a figure that includes people who moved off the team inside the window. Both numbers are real and they count different things.

06. Was the tracker running from January, or did you backfill? +

I backfilled. The app went live in April. January, February and March were loaded in afterwards, by me, writing SQL against Supabase by hand from the records that already existed.

So two real things on this site are counting different things. The 1,324 rows cover January to April, and the first three months of that window are records I entered, not advisors logging themselves. Daily self-logging by the advisors describes April onward.

Both are true. I would rather split them out than let one number quietly carry the other.

07. How much AI is actually in these projects? +

Less than the word usually implies, and I would rather say so directly than let it slide.

The Sales Tracker and Move-Out generator contain no ML/AI models. Homebase contains an opt-in model path behind a feature flag (USE_LLM_SCOPE=true), while its default path is a deterministic template builder.

The real AI work was evaluation: running structured usability sessions and analyzing voice agent call output logs to catch failure modes before production. The session count is not recorded in the repo, so I do not claim a number.

08. How much of this did AI write? +

A lot of it, and I would rather say that than have you assume otherwise. I built with AI. I would not have been able to do it without AI. The tracker is still running.

What I will not claim is that AI did the thinking. Vibe coding is very easy right now and it is not going to generate a product for you. I had to understand how the product needed to work first: what makes two rows the same event, what a supervisor actually needs on a dashboard, what a field team on phones can and cannot do. You need the knowledge and your own input, or it does not work.

And no, I am not going to trust it 100%. That is not how it works. I check what it hands me, because I am the one who has to answer for what the numbers say.

09. Were 29 rows rejected by the pipeline? +

Removed, not rejected. The 29 were valid rows that collapsed as duplicates against the key. Nothing failed validation and nothing was thrown away as bad data: 1,324 rows in, 0 rejected.

The distinction matters because "rejected" would imply the export was malformed. It was not. It was duplicated, which is a quieter problem and a more dangerous one, because every row looks individually plausible.

10. Why do you say 3 markets instead of 3 stores? +

Markets, not doors. The data holds 3 distinct store values at city level, Cairo, Sharm and Hurghada, so coverage is stated at market level. I do not claim a door count, because the export does not support one.

11. Did it make month-end reporting faster? +

Yes, and I cannot prove by how much, so I am not going to put a figure on it.

No cycle time measured. Before the tracker, month-end totals arrived weeks late. After it, daily numbers were available. But no before-and-after turnaround was ever logged, so any specific improvement I quoted would be an estimate dressed as a measurement.

12. The move-out sheet says 21 of 32. Your site says 20 of 31. +

The sheet is counting rows, not objects. A COUNTIF includes one synthetic Dining Set Bundle row whose table and chairs are also listed individually, so the bundle is counted twice.

The honest figure is 20 of 31. That is the number on the site. AED 8,400 recovered against AED 10,688 paid for those items, a 78.6% recovery rate, measured against what was paid for the items sold and not against total apartment spend.

13. What have you not done? +

I have not shipped a machine learning model to production. I have not worked at engineering scale: the largest user base here is 18 people, and the largest data volume is a few thousand rows. I have not run a team of engineers.

Homebase's RERA compliance layer is specified in the README and explicitly listed as out of scope there, so it is a spec and not a product. I have not built it.
