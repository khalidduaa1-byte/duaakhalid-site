QUESTIONS & CAVEATS

Your questions answered directly.

Written by me. These are the questions that actually come up when someone reads the site, including the uncomfortable ones about how small the numbers are and what the AI work actually is.

01. What did you actually build across these projects? +

Beauty Advisor Sales Tracker: A PWA for Dolce & Gabbana Beauty in travel retail MEA where 18 advisors log daily sales, backed by an ingest pipeline that de-duplicates exports before KPIs are calculated.

LVMH Sell-in Tool: Internal product owner for Givenchy & Kenzo sell-in decision support tool, cutting ordering time from 45 mins to 5 mins across 30+ locations.

Dolce & Gabbana Account Health: Benchmarking layer tracking a 13-market portfolio that flags at-risk accounts early.

Move-Out Sale Site: A Python generator producing a buyer-facing catalog and internal profit tracker from a single source list.

02. What is the dedup key and why does it matter? +

The dedup key defines what makes two rows the same event rather than two events. Here it was advisor + date + store + shift.

Duplicate rows double-count sales and inflate Days Worked, distorting every daily KPI. Settling this key upfront ensured that across 1,324 ingested rows, 29 duplicates were safely removed and zero rows were rejected.

03. Isn't 18 beauty advisors a small number? +

Yes, 18 is a small number and I am not going to dress it up. What matters is that nobody was required to use it.

The tracker was optional. All 18 advisors across Cairo, Sharm, and Hurghada logged into it daily because it solved a real friction point. Mandated tools show compliance; optional tools that teams open daily show real product adoption.

18 live, about 20 onboarded. The January to April export holds 18 advisors. Onboarding and training covered roughly 20 field users, a figure that includes people who moved off the team inside the window. Both numbers are real and they count different things.

04. How much AI is actually in these projects? +

Less than the word usually implies, and I would rather say so directly than let it slide.

The Sales Tracker and Move-Out generator contain no ML/AI models. Homebase contains an opt-in model path behind a feature flag (USE_LLM_SCOPE=true), while its default path is a deterministic template builder.

The real AI work was evaluation: running structured usability sessions and analyzing voice agent call output logs to catch failure modes before production. The session count is not recorded in the repo, so I do not claim a number.

05. Were 29 rows rejected by the pipeline? +

Removed, not rejected. The 29 were valid rows that collapsed as duplicates against the key. Nothing failed validation and nothing was thrown away as bad data: 1,324 rows in, 0 rejected.

The distinction matters because "rejected" would imply the export was malformed. It was not. It was duplicated, which is a quieter problem and a more dangerous one, because every row looks individually plausible.

06. Why do you say 3 markets instead of 3 stores? +

Markets, not doors. The data holds 3 distinct store values at city level, Cairo, Sharm and Hurghada, so coverage is stated at market level. I do not claim a door count, because the export does not support one.

07. Did it make month-end reporting faster? +

Yes, and I cannot prove by how much, so I am not going to put a figure on it.

No cycle time measured. Before the tracker, month-end totals arrived weeks late. After it, daily numbers were available. But no before-and-after turnaround was ever logged, so any specific improvement I quoted would be an estimate dressed as a measurement.

08. The move-out sheet says 21 of 32. Your site says 20 of 31. +

The sheet is counting rows, not objects. A COUNTIF includes one synthetic Dining Set Bundle row whose table and chairs are also listed individually, so the bundle is counted twice.

The honest figure is 20 of 31. That is the number on the site. AED 8,400 recovered against AED 10,688 paid for those items, a 78.6% recovery rate, measured against what was paid for the items sold and not against total apartment spend.

09. What have you not done? +

I have not shipped a machine learning model to production. I have not worked at engineering scale: the largest user base here is 18 people, and the largest data volume is a few thousand rows. I have not run a team of engineers.

Homebase's RERA compliance layer is specified in the README and explicitly listed as out of scope there, so it is a spec and not a product. I have not built it.

What I have done is take a broken operational input, decide what a valid record is, and get a field team in another country to work the new way without being told to. That is the part I would defend.

10. Is anything on your own sites broken right now? +

Yes. The move-out catalog renders "0 items available" from a hardcoded placeholder that the JavaScript overwrites on a normal load. It should read 11. It is not sold out.

It lives in a separate repository from this site. I would rather list it here than have you find it and wonder what else is unchecked.
